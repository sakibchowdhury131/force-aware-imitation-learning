1. PROBLEM STATEMENT AND OVERALL ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Kinova Jaco2 has no dedicated force/torque sensor at the wrist or
end-effector. The only signal available is joint torque, read via the SDK
(raw torque and firmware "gravity-free" torque). The full joint-torque
balance for an n-DOF manipulator is:

    tau = M(q) qddot + C(q, qdot) qdot + G(q) + tau_friction + J^T(q) F_tip     (1)

where:
  tau           -- measured joint torque (6,)
  M(q)          -- joint-space mass/inertia matrix
  C(q, qdot)    -- Coriolis/centripetal matrix
  G(q)          -- gravity torque
  tau_friction  -- joint friction (viscous + Coulomb)
  J(q)          -- geometric Jacobian (6x6, EEF frame)
  F_tip         -- external wrench at the end-effector (the signal of interest)

Recovering F_tip requires modeling and subtracting every other term. This
project builds that model in layers, each calibrated independently and
verified before being composed with the next:

    raw torque (GetAngularForce)
      -> GetAngularForceGravityFree                     [firmware gravity matrix]
      -> - Y_g(q) @ phi                                 [software gravity regressor]
      -> - G_res(q)                                     [gravity residual NN]
      -> - Y_full(q,qdot,qddot) @ pi                     [mass/Coriolis regressor]
      -> tau_ext  ->  torque_to_wrench()  ->  F_ext       (the quantity used everywhere downstream)

Once F_ext can be estimated accurately and quickly (10 Hz is the deployment
loop rate), the pipeline is used two ways:
  (a) OFFLINE, to build per-frame force labels for imitation-learning
      training data, by re-executing recorded human demonstrations on the
      robot itself ("action replay") and reading joint torque while it moves
      (Section 4);
  (b) ONLINE, during deployment, to feed a live force reading into a
      force-conditioned policy's observation, and to close an admittance
      control loop around the policy's predicted force (Sections 5-7).

The core hardware constraint that shapes the whole design: direct
joint-torque control on this Jaco2 was found unreliable earlier in the
project (the SDK API reports success but the arm does not actually move
under torque commands, and it has needed a power cycle to recover). All
control in this project therefore stays in **Cartesian position control**;
"force control" here means *using a force estimate to modulate a position
command*, i.e. admittance control (Section 6), not direct torque control.


2. GRAVITY ESTIMATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Gravity is calibrated in two independent layers stacked on top of each
other: a firmware-level fit (Kinova's own routine) and a software-level
correction on top of that (first linear, then a residual neural network on
top of the linear fit).

2.1 Firmware gravity matrix (calibrate_firmware_gravity.py)
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    Uses Kinova's own `RunGravityZEstimationSequence` + `SetGravityOptimalZParam`
    SDK routines -- an autonomous calibration sequence that moves the arm
    through a set of poses and fits the firmware's internal gravity model.

    - First attempt: 93.8% reduction in residual force, but the fitted
      parameters destabilized actuator J6 (hardware fault, joystick red
      lights, one-direction-only rotation until a power cycle). Suspected
      root cause: uncalibrated torque sensors at the hardware level, an
      undocumented precondition of this SDK routine. Result discarded, not
      reused.
    - Second attempt (clean, currently in use): mean ||F|| 21.41N -> 1.69N
      (92.1% reduction) across 8 calibration poses, confirmed fault-free.
        - Held-out check (6 new poses, same region): 1.81-1.87N residual --
          generalizes well near its own calibration poses.
        - Out-of-region check (pose with J2=111.7 deg, calibration spanned
          ~183-223 deg): degraded to 6.51N -- a real generalization gap
          outside the calibrated joint-angle region.
    - Persistence: firmware gravity parameters do NOT survive a power cycle
      (confirmed twice). `apply_saved_gravity_params()` must be called once
      per session; this is wired automatically into the startup of 8
      scripts (diag_contact.py, calibrate_contact_baseline.py,
      diag_joint_torques.py, replay_episode.py, 07_deploy.py,
      teach_by_hand.py, quick_pose_check.py, test_residual_repeatability.py).

2.2 Software linear gravity regressor -- mathematical model
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    Even after the firmware fit, a smooth, pose-dependent residual (~1-2.5N
    mapped to the EEF) survives in free space with no contact -- the
    firmware's internal link mass/COM parameters don't perfectly match the
    physical arm. This residual is itself a gravity torque, and gravity
    torque is exactly LINEAR in a per-link parameter set (m, m*cx, m*cy,
    m*cz):

        tau_residual(q) = Y_g(q) @ phi                                      (2)

    phi packs, per link, [m, m*cx, m*cy, m*cz] (4 parameters/link x 6 links
    = 24 total) -- these are best-fit *corrections*, not the arm's true
    inertial parameters (static gravity data alone can't identify all of
    them; the fit is ridge-regularized).

    Y_g(q) (the "gravity regressor") is derived analytically, not learned.
    For link i (outboard of joint j, i.e. i >= j), gravity torque at joint j
    is the sum over such links of the moment of that link's gravitational
    force about joint j's axis:

        Y_g[j, 4i]      = z_j . ( (p_i - o_j) x a0 )            (mass column)
        Y_g[j, 4i+1:4i+4] = -z_j^T . skew(a0) . R_i               (m*c columns)

    where a0 = -G_BASE is the fictitious base acceleration representing
    gravity, z_j/o_j are joint j's axis/origin, p_i is link i's origin, and
    R_i is link i's orientation -- all from the same forward-kinematics
    chain (`fk_link_frames`) used everywhere else in the pipeline.
    G_BASE = [0, 0, -9.81] (base +Z is "up" for this upright-mounted arm;
    confirmed empirically, not just by internal self-consistency).

    Full model at this stage, in subtraction order:

        tau_gf = Y_g(q) @ phi + [M(q)qddot + C(q,qdot)qdot] + tau_ext        (3)

    (the middle term is near-zero in the quasi-static regime used for clean
    force reads, and is itself modeled separately in Section 3).

2.3 Software linear gravity regressor -- random poses vs. task poses
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    (a) Random poses (calibrate_gravity_residual.py): phi fit by ridge
        regression from poses generated by perturbing around known-safe
        anchor configurations, unrelated to any specific task.
          - 24 poses over 3 accumulating batches: mean ||F|| 6.08N -> 5.32N
            (12.5% reduction), but inconsistent -- 6 of 24 poses got WORSE
            (up to -63%), and the reduction DECLINED with more data across
            batches (27.4% -> 20.9% -> 12.5%).

    (b) Task poses (collect_task_poses.py): same linear regressor, but
        poses taken directly from real pastaTransfer4 episode trajectories
        (the actual poses the deployed policy visits).
          - Mixed test (24 random + 9 task poses, evaluated on the 9 task
            poses): 5.94N -> 3.34N (43.7% reduction), all 9 improved (37-51%
            each), no outliers.
          - Task-only training (40 poses from 8 episodes, no random poses):
            6.56N -> 1.43N (78.2% reduction), stable across all 4
            accumulating batches (79.7% -> 78.2%), every pose improved, no
            decline with more data.
          - Held-out validation (15 poses from 5 entirely different
            episodes, never used in training): 5.99N -> 1.51N (74.8%
            reduction), all 15 improved (59-89% each), landing at the
            expected ~1-2N noise floor.

    CONCLUSION: calibrating on real task poses is dramatically better and
    more consistent than generic randomized poses -- the improvement is
    concentrated specifically in the region the poses cover (confirmed: the
    combined random+task fit, re-evaluated on the original 24 random poses,
    still only reached ~15.7% reduction there).

    Trusted artifact: data/gravity_phi_task_only.npy (fit) +
    data/gravity_calibration_records_task_only.npz (the 40 training poses).

2.4 Gravity-residual neural network (fit_gravity_residual_nn.py)
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    A small NN, G_res(q) -- input sin(q), cos(q) ONLY (pose, never velocity
    or acceleration) -- fit ON TOP OF the trusted linear fit
    (gravity_phi_task_only.npy), using exclusively static/near-zero-velocity
    data (so it can never be confused with, or contaminate, the separate
    mass/Coriolis residual work in Section 3).

    Architecture: nn.Sequential(Linear(12, hidden), ReLU, Linear(hidden,
    hidden), ReLU, Linear(hidden, 6)) i.e. sin/cos(q) (12-dim) -> hidden ->
    hidden -> 6 joint-torque corrections. Trained on 40 dedicated
    calibration poses plus near-static stretches mined from already-
    collected motion episodes (extract_static_stretches, no new hardware
    time needed).

    - Re-fitting the LINEAR model with more poses barely helped (near-static
      residual only dropped 0.81 -> 0.77 N*m) -- the fixed 24-parameter
      linear functional form, not lack of data, was the bottleneck.
    - The nonlinear NN did far better:
        - 65 static samples (40 dedicated + near-static motion stretches):
          1.53N -> 0.49N (68.2% train), 1.93N -> 1.13N (41.3% held-out, 18
          samples from 3 untouched episodes).
        - Scaled to 190 static samples (30 episodes): 1.50N -> 0.55N (63.0%
          train), 1.93N -> 1.36N (29.3% held-out -- dip likely small-sample
          noise at n=18, not a concern).

    CONCLUSION: for gravity, a nonlinear pose-only correction recovers far
    more of the residual than the linear model can, regardless of how much
    data the linear model gets -- a genuine, uncontested win for the NN.
    True residual here (kinematic/sensor calibration error) has no reason
    to be linear in anything.

    Trusted artifact: data/gravity_residual_nn.pt


3. MASS/CORIOLIS (RIGID-BODY DYNAMICS) RESIDUAL ESTIMATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Why the naive per-link parametrization fails -- the bilinearity problem
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    contact_detector.py's original `rnea_no_gravity` parametrizes each link
    by (mass, COM) SEPARATELY. That makes M(q)qddot + C(q,qdot)qdot
    BILINEAR, not linear, in those parameters (mass multiplies a term that
    already depends on COM) -- it cannot be turned into a regressor by
    simply overriding those three quantities independently, the way
    gravity_regressor could.

3.2 The barycentric reparametrization -- exact linear regressor
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    Standard fix (Khalil & Dombre; Featherstone's spatial-inertia
    formulation; the same trick gravity_regressor already used, extended):
    reparametrize each link i by the barycentric triple:

        mass_i                      (1 parameter)
        h_i = mass_i * com_i        (3 parameters, "first moment" -- same
                                      quantity gravity_regressor's m*c columns use)
        J_i = inertia about the link's OWN REFERENCE-FRAME ORIGIN, not COM
                                     (6 independent parameters, symmetric tensor)

    Because the kinematic quantities (a_i, omega_i, alpha_i) never depend on
    the CURRENT link's own dynamic parameters (only on upstream motion and
    pure geometry), the force/moment equations become linear and DECOUPLED
    in (mass_i, h_i, J_i):

        f_i = mass_i * a_i + alpha_i x h_i + omega_i x (omega_i x h_i)       (4)
        n_i = J_i . alpha_i + omega_i x (J_i . omega_i) + h_i x a_i          (5)

    (moments taken about the link's reference-frame origin directly, not
    COM -- hence the extra h_i x a_i term that the COM-based rnea_no_gravity
    does not have).

    This makes the whole equation EXACTLY LINEAR in 10 parameters/link (60
    total) plus a viscous+Coulomb friction term (2 parameters/joint, 12
    total) -- 72 parameters overall:

        M(q) qddot + C(q,qdot) qdot [+ friction] = Y_full(q,qdot,qddot) @ pi   (6)

    pi packs, per link: [mass, hx, hy, hz, Jxx, Jyy, Jzz, Jxy, Jxz, Jyz],
    plus [viscous_j, coulomb_j] per joint appended at the end. Y_full is
    built by UNIT PERTURBATION of the reparametrized RNEA (`rnea_full`):
    since rnea_full is exactly linear in each parameter by construction,
    evaluating it with one parameter set to 1 and all others to 0 gives
    EXACTLY that parameter's regressor column (not a finite-difference
    approximation -- linear functions have exact, step-size-independent
    "derivatives" this way). Friction columns:

        Y_full[i, viscous_col]  = qdot_i                          (viscous)
        Y_full[i, coulomb_col]  = sign(qdot_i)  (0 if |qdot_i| < 1e-6)   (Coulomb)

    Verification performed before fitting anything (self-test in
    contact_detector.py's __main__):
      - rnea_full at the nominal (converted) link parameters reproduces
        rnea_no_gravity's original output to ~1e-16.
      - full_dynamics_regressor's first 4 columns per link, evaluated at
        qdot=qddot=0 with the gravity-equivalent base acceleration, reduce
        to gravity_regressor exactly.
      - M(q), built column-by-column via unit qddot with qdot=0, is
        symmetric and positive-definite (a necessary property of any valid
        rigid-body inertia matrix).
      - tau is exactly zero at qdot=qddot=0 for any q.
    Later optimized (~1.9x speedup: forward kinematics computed once per
    sample and reused across the 72 unit-perturbation calls, instead of
    recomputed 72 times) with no change to any result (re-verified to the
    same precision).

3.3 First attempt: a confounded neural-network comparison
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    Excitation data (real q_dot/q_ddot needed, unlike the deliberately slow
    quasi-static replays used for clean contact-force reading elsewhere):
    `replay_episode.py --no_wait_convergence --speed_scale 0.5
    --arm_trans_speed 0.08`, 6 training + 3 held-out pastaTransfer4 episodes.

    Target: gravity-free torque - gravity_regressor(q)@phi -
    rnea_no_gravity(q,qdot,qddot).

        Method                         Train reduction   Held-out reduction
        ----------------------------   ----------------   -------------------
        Linear regressor (72 params)         4.2%               3.6%
        Neural network (unconstrained)      39.0%              34.1%

    The NN looked like a huge win. IT WASN'T. A direct check -- feeding the
    NN real held-out poses with qdot=qddot=0 substituted, where a genuine
    M(q)qddot+C(q,qdot)qdot effect is mathematically required to be exactly
    zero -- showed the NN still output ~1.48N, almost as large as its
    entire real-world correction. Confirmed independently: near-static
    samples (|qdot|<5 deg/s) carried nearly as much residual (0.81 N*m) as
    fast samples (1.10 N*m), meaning most of the "residual" wasn't
    dynamics-dependent at all.

    ROOT CAUSE: the NN's input included q directly with no constraint
    forcing zero output at rest, so it was quietly patching leftover
    pose-dependent GRAVITY error (Section 2) that the regressor is
    mathematically incapable of touching (Y_full(q,qdot,qddot) is exactly
    zero whenever qdot=qddot=0, by construction). Not a fair comparison.

3.4 The fix: cleaning the target, constraining the network
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    Two parts, both required:
      1. DATA FIX: subtract the gravity-residual NN's correction (Section
         2.4) from the target BEFORE either dynamics model sees it, so
         neither model is asked to explain pose-only leftover error as if
         it were dynamics.
      2. ARCHITECTURE FIX: rebuilt the NN as two physically-shaped heads
         instead of one monolithic network (`ConstrainedResidualNet` in
         fit_dynamics_nn_constrained.py):

             M_res(q)      -- input sin(q),cos(q) only -- outputs a
                               symmetric 6x6 matrix
             C_res(q,qdot) -- input sin(q),cos(q),qdot -- outputs a
                               general 6x6 matrix

             tau_residual = M_res(q) @ qddot + C_res(q, qdot) @ qdot         (7)

         Both outputs are multiplied by qddot/qdot respectively, so the
         combined prediction is EXACTLY ZERO whenever qdot=qddot=0, for ANY
         network weights -- the same hard constraint the regressor already
         had, enforced by the multiplication itself, not by training.
         Verified directly: max|prediction| = 0.00e+00 at synthetic rest
         points.

3.5 Regressor vs. constrained NN -- final results at scale
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    Corrected comparison, 6 training episodes:

        Method              Train reduction   Held-out reduction
        -----------------   ----------------   -------------------
        Regressor                 5.0%               4.7%
        Constrained NN            16.3%              6.8%

    (Baseline itself, before either dynamics model does anything, dropped
    from 2.11/2.22N to 1.61/1.71N train/held-out just from the gravity fix
    -- ~24%.) The constrained NN still edged out the regressor on held-out
    data, but its train-to-held-out drop (16.3% -> 6.8%, more than half)
    signaled it was partly memorizing the 6 specific training episodes.

    Scaled up to 30 training episodes (every pastaTransfer4 episode with
    tracked data except the 3 held-out ones, which stayed completely
    untouched throughout). Gravity-residual NN refit on 190 static samples
    (up from 65). Dynamics target and both models rebuilt from scratch
    (37,671 training samples, up from 7,514):

        Method            Train (6ep)  Held-out (6ep)  Train (30ep)  Held-out (30ep)
        ---------------   -----------  --------------  ------------  ---------------
        Regressor            5.0%          4.7%             4.9%          5.2%
        Constrained NN       16.3%         6.8%             9.1%          5.3%

    With 5x more training data, the NN's train-set number FELL (16.3% ->
    9.1%, it can no longer memorize 6 episodes as easily) while its
    held-out number barely moved (6.8% -> 5.3%). The train/held-out gap
    shrank from 9.5 points to 3.8. REGRESSOR AND NN ARE NOW WITHIN NOISE OF
    EACH OTHER ON HELD-OUT DATA (5.2% vs 5.3% -- matching to 3 decimal
    places in mean and std of the raw evaluation).

    This convergence is the real finding: the true recoverable
    mass/Coriolis effect in this workspace is small (~5%), and both a
    72-parameter linear model and a flexible constrained NN land on the
    same number once given enough data to stop overfitting. The NN's
    earlier apparent 34-39% advantage was almost entirely a
    data-scarcity/confound artifact, not a genuine capability gap.

3.6 Bottom line on gravity/dynamics modeling
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    - GRAVITY RESIDUAL: use the NN (fit_gravity_residual_nn.py). Real,
      substantial, uncontested improvement (63-68% train, 29-41% held-out)
      over the linear model -- the true residual there (kinematic/sensor
      calibration error) has no reason to be linear in anything.
    - MASS/CORIOLIS RESIDUAL: use the REGRESSOR
      (fit_dynamics_regressor.py / full_dynamics_regressor). Statistically
      tied with the NN in achievable accuracy (~5% either way), but far
      simpler, numerically stable across every configuration tried, fully
      inspectable, and mathematically guaranteed to never predict an
      unphysical result -- because M(q)qddot+C(q,qdot)qdot is EXACTLY the
      linear structure the regressor already encodes; there is little
      nonlinear-in-q structure left for a network to find.
    - LAYERING THAT WORKS, END TO END: firmware gravity matrix -> software
      gravity regressor (gravity_phi_task_only.npy) -> gravity residual NN
      (gravity_residual_nn.pt) -> nominal RNEA (rnea_no_gravity) ->
      mass/Coriolis regressor (dynamics_residual_pi.npy) -> remainder is
      tau_ext, mapped to an end-effector wrench via torque_to_wrench.

    Sanity-check numbers (no contact): the final-estimate tier should read
    ~1-3N, occasionally briefly higher during fast motion -- this is the
    established sensor/model noise floor for this arm, not a defect. A real
    applied push looks like a clean ramp from that floor up to however hard
    you push (30N+ seen for a firm push in testing) and back down over
    roughly half a second -- sharp isolated single-sample spikes with no
    ramp are more likely noise than real contact.


4. END-EFFECTOR EXTERNAL FORCE ESTIMATION VIA ACTION REPLAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Why replay, not the human demonstration, is the source of force data
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    The human hand holding the spoon during demonstration recording carries
    NO force sensor -- there is no way to measure contact force during a
    bare-hand demo. The only way to harvest force data for this task is to
    have the ROBOT itself re-execute ("replay") the tracked human
    trajectory and read its own joint-torque sensors while doing so
    (`replay_episode.py`). This is why the training data source for every
    force-conditioned model in this project is REPLAY images/torque, not
    the original human-demonstration images -- pose labels are shared
    between both (replay re-executes the exact tracked trajectory, so a
    given frame_id's tool_poses_base.npz pose label is correct for both),
    but force only exists on the replay side.

    Two replay motion modes exist:
      - Quasi-static (default): stops and settles at each of ~100-150
        waypoints (~70s total) -- deliberately slow, for clean contact-force
        readings, at the cost of looking jittery/stop-start.
      - Continuous (`--no_wait_convergence --speed_scale 0.5
        --arm_trans_speed 0.08`): smooth motion (~20-25s total), needed to
        generate real qdot/qddot for exciting the dynamics models
        (Section 3), at the cost of more real inertial noise in the force
        reading.

4.2 The full three-tier force-recovery pipeline (equations)
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    Implemented identically in three places: diag_replay_forces.py (live,
    during a replay), analyze_replay_full.py (post-hoc, from a saved log --
    this is what actually produces every training label used in this
    project), and 07_deploy_force.py (live, during deployment, once the bug
    in Section 4.5 was fixed). Per timestep i:

        F_fw          = torque_to_wrench(q_i, gf_i)                                    (tier 1: firmware-only)

        g_res_lin     = gravity_regressor(q_i) @ phi
        g_res_nn      = GravityResidualNet(q_i)
        tau_after_g   = gf_i - g_res_lin - g_res_nn
        F_grav        = torque_to_wrench(q_i, tau_after_g)                              (tier 2: + gravity, regressor+NN)

        qddot_i       = VelocityDifferentiator.update(qdot_i, t_i)   [smoothed finite difference]
        dyn_pred      = full_dynamics_regressor(q_i, qdot_i, qddot_i) @ pi
        tau_final     = tau_after_g - dyn_pred
        F_final       = torque_to_wrench(q_i, tau_final)                                (tier 3: + mass/Coriolis, FINAL ESTIMATE)

    where gf_i is the firmware's GetAngularForceGravityFree reading. F_final
    is "the number that matters" -- the pure external wrench at the EEF,
    base frame -- used for every downstream force label and live reading in
    this project.

    torque_to_wrench maps joint torque to an EEF wrench via the Jacobian
    transpose:

        tau = J^T(q) F   =>   F = pinv(J^T(q)) tau                                      (8)

    Near a kinematic singularity, cond(J) blows up and plain pinv(J^T)
    amplifies torque noise without bound (confirmed on hardware: a J5=180
    deg spherical-wrist singularity turned a real ~1 N*m torque error into
    a reported multi-million-Newton "force"). Two safeguards:
      - damping > 0 uses damped least squares instead of pinv -- the
        Tikhonov-regularized solution to
        min ||J^T F - tau||^2 + damping^2 ||F||^2, i.e.
        F = (J J^T + damping^2 I)^-1 J tau. (damping=0 recovers plain-pinv
        behavior exactly, so existing callers were unaffected when this was
        added.)
      - A warning prints whenever cond(J) exceeds a threshold (1e4 default),
        regardless of damping.

4.3 From dense replay logs to per-frame training labels
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    Force preprocessing, per episode (05_train_replay_force.py):
      1. Load the DENSE replay force log (~47 Hz, replay/replay_full_forces.npz)
         -- NOT the already-sparse per-waypoint log
         (replay_forces_per_frame.npz, ~150 samples over ~60s, ~2.5Hz
         average, right at the Nyquist edge for a 2Hz filter and too coarse
         to filter meaningfully).
      2. TARE: subtract the mean force over the first `--tare_window_s`
         seconds. Pose-dependent gravity-residual bias varies episode-to-
         episode by several Newtons even at rest (confirmed empirically
         across episodes 020/021/025/030/035/037) -- an untared force is
         partly recalibration noise, not contact signal.
      3. LOW-PASS FILTER at `--force_cutoff_hz` (default 2.0 Hz, matching
         plot_episode_forces.py's chosen visualization cutoff), via
         scipy.signal.filtfilt -- offline/non-causal (zero phase lag), fine
         for a training-label pass (not a real-time loop).
      4. For each frame_id used by the dataset window sampler, look up that
         frame's timestamp (torque_log.npz's frame_id -> t mapping, already
         nearest-matched to the dense log with sub-20ms error) and take the
         nearest tared+filtered dense sample. This is the frame's 3D force
         label, used both as a past-observation feature and a
         future-prediction target.

    Note on which episodes have force data: 36 of PastaTransfer_force's 37
    episodes have replay force logs (episodes 001-019 never had
    `--capture_camera` replay run; "021-037" was a training-DATA-SUBSET
    naming choice made for an earlier, unrelated policy-architecture batch,
    not a force-log-availability boundary).

4.4 Live force estimation at deployment time
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    Same three-tier chain, run once per 10Hz control tick (100ms budget).
    `full_dynamics_regressor` costs ~25ms/call (confirmed via
    benchmark_force_admittance.py's stage-timing test), i.e. ~40Hz max --
    fine at 10Hz (uses <40% of one tick), but explicitly too slow for a
    100Hz admittance loop (a separate, faster streaming architecture,
    deploy_streaming.py, exists for that case and simply omits this stage).
    qddot is estimated live via a persistent `VelocityDifferentiator`
    (smoothed finite difference of qdot over time, `--qddot_smoothing`
    default 0.3, matching the training-label pipeline's default).

4.5 A real pipeline bug: live force vs. training-label force mismatch
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    Found 2026-07-13. `07_deploy_force.py`'s live `read_F_raw` was copied
    from `deploy_streaming.py`'s 100Hz loop, which explicitly OMITTED the
    mass/Coriolis dynamics-regressor stage (tier 3) for being "too slow" at
    100Hz. That constraint was never re-checked for this script's 10Hz loop
    (where the regressor easily fits the tick budget). NET EFFECT: every
    live force reading, in every deployment run up to this point, was on a
    DIFFERENT correction chain than what the model was actually trained on
    -- training labels were fully dynamics-compensated (tier 3), live reads
    stopped at tier 2.

    Fixed: added `get_qdot_deg`/`GetAngularVelocity` SDK binding, a
    persistent `VelocityDifferentiator` for live qddot estimation, and the
    missing dynamics-regressor subtraction, matching
    `analyze_replay_full.py`'s chain exactly. New `--dynamics_pi` /
    `--qddot_smoothing` args (defaults match the training-label pipeline).
    All admittance-control experimental results in Section 7 below were
    collected AFTER this fix (results collected before it, from earlier the
    same day, are not directly comparable and are called out as such where
    they appear in the source notes).


5. FORCE-CONDITIONED POLICY ARCHITECTURES AND TRAINING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Base policy: DDPM diffusion policy (existing `DiffusionPolicyNet`,
`policy_common.py`), dual-camera image observation, `action_horizon=16`,
trained on REPLAY images (matching what deployment's live camera sees --
the robot's own gripper doing the task, no human hand to mask out).

`05_train_replay_force.py` extends the replay-image dataset
(`05_train_replay.py`) from 9D pose observation/action to 12D
[pose9, force3], in two variants:

5.1 Combined variant (force is both observed and predicted)
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    - Observation: past 12D [pose, force] proprioception.
    - Action target: future 12D [pose, force], predicted JOINTLY over the
      same action_horizon.
    - Loss: per-dimension weighted noise-prediction MSE. Pose dims weighted
      1.0, force dims weighted 0.2 (`--force_loss_weight`, default) --
      force is a noisier, more contact-geometry-sensitive signal across
      demonstrations than pose (two demos of "the same" phase can have
      different contact force even with similar poses), so it stays
      auxiliary; pose prediction remains the dominant training signal.
    - `policy_common.py`'s `DiffusionPolicyNet`/`MaxAbsNormalizer` needed
      ZERO changes (already dimension-generic via `action_dim`/`proprio_dim`
      constructor params) -- only the dataset/training script changed.
    - Checkpoint metadata stores `proprio_dim=12`, `action_dim=12`,
      `predicts_force=True`, `force_in_proprio=True`.

5.2 Conditioning-only variant (force observed, never predicted)
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    `--no_predict_force` flag: force still feeds the observation (proprio
    stays 12D), but the action target is pure 9D pose -- the model is never
    asked to predict future force, only better positions. Isolates "does
    force-conditioning improve pose prediction" from "can this model also
    predict force" (the combined variant's force-prediction accuracy was
    found to generalize poorly to a held-out high-drift episode -- see
    5.3). Checkpoint metadata: `proprio_dim=12`, `action_dim=9`,
    `predicts_force=False`, `force_in_proprio=True` (this last field is
    stored SEPARATELY from `predicts_force` precisely so the deploy script
    can build the right-shaped proprio observation regardless of variant).

    Deployment-side: this variant runs through the ordinary position
    controller with no admittance correction available (there is no
    predicted future force to use as a reference) -- it can only ever
    inform pose prediction, never modulate the control law directly.

    Training data for both variants: episodes 001-030 only (36 episodes had
    force logs in total; 031-037 excluded -- see 5.3, they are a high-drift
    outlier cluster), episode 020 held out for validation. Both trained
    concurrently; val loss bottomed around epoch 100-110 for both (combined
    0.0422, cond-only 0.0449), then rose (standard overfitting pattern seen
    across every policy trained in this project) -- manually stopped at
    epoch ~290, `policy_epoch0100.pt` used as the best-available checkpoint
    for both (saved every 100 epochs; epoch 100 sits very close to the true
    minimum for both).

5.3 Offline force-prediction accuracy and the episode drift investigation
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    `eval_force_prediction.py` (reuses the training dataset class + real
    DDPM inference, no robot needed) compares predicted vs. actual recorded
    force on a held-out episode, against a naive "assume force stays
    constant" baseline:

        Episode   Model MAE   Baseline MAE   Result
        -------   ---------   ------------   ------------------------------------
        037       1.754N      0.955N         Model WORSE by 83.8% (worst on Fx:
                                              2.946 vs 0.696N). Error flat/bad from
                                              horizon step 1 onward -- signature of
                                              systematic mismatch, not "hard to
                                              predict far ahead."
        020       0.393N      0.866N         Model BEATS baseline by 54.6%, wins
                                              every axis and every horizon step.
                                              (Was in-training-set for the run that
                                              produced this number -- not a clean
                                              generalization test on its own.)

    Investigation: checked "drift" (mean force in the last 3s of an
    episode's recording minus the first 3s) across all 37 episodes with
    replay force logs:

        Episodes    Drift magnitude
        ---------   --------------------------------------------
        001-030     mean 1.78N, median 1.57N, range [0.53, 3.39]
        031-037     mean 5.12N, median 5.37N, range [3.24, 6.55] -- 2-3x higher

    Cross-referenced recording timestamps: 025-030 recorded 15:15-15:48
    (2026-07-10), an 11-minute gap, then 031-037 recorded 15:59-16:47, same
    day/session. The drift jump lines up almost exactly with that gap --
    consistent with a session-cumulative (most likely thermal, motor/joint
    heating over a continuous multi-hour session) effect rather than random
    per-episode noise. Episode 037 (the validation episode in the first
    training run) sits in this anomalous cluster, plausibly explaining its
    poor generalization result above more than any fundamental flaw in the
    modeling approach.

    Live check, same day: deployed the existing position-only (non-force)
    checkpoint live and measured the CURRENT live force drift over a
    verified, complete 95.3s run: 1.36N -- squarely in the 001-030 "normal"
    range, nowhere near the 031-037 cluster. Confirms the anomaly was
    specific to that one 2026-07-10 recording session, not a persistent
    property of the robot's current calibration state.

    Response: both training variants (5.1/5.2) were retrained on episodes
    001-030 only, excluding the high-drift cluster (see 5.2 above).


6. ADMITTANCE CONTROL: THEORY AND IMPLEMENTATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 Connection to Modern Robotics Ch. 11 (force/impedance control)
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    Modern Robotics (Lynch & Park) Section 11.5's force-control law commands
    JOINT TORQUE directly:

        tau = g~(theta) + J^T(theta) [ Fd + Kfp Fe + Kfi INT(Fe dt) - Kdamp V ]        (9)

    requiring reliable torque-control mode. NOT implementable here -- direct
    torque control on this Jaco2 was already found unreliable earlier in
    the project (API reports success but control fails; needed a power
    cycle), so all force work stays in position control.

    Section 11.7 (Impedance/Admittance Control) is the textbook's own
    prescribed alternative for exactly this situation, defined by the
    general second-order relationship (Eq. 11.62):

        m x_ddot + b x_dot + k x = f                                                   (10)

    or, in transfer-function form, Y(s) = X(s)/F(s) -- force in, position
    out. What this project built (below) is the STIFFNESS-ONLY special case
    of this equation (m=0, b=0), which is literally the structure already
    arrived at independently before this reading was done.

6.2 The implemented control law
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    Implemented in `07_deploy_force.py` (`--admittance` flag, off by
    default; requires a `predicts_force` checkpoint). Per control tick:

        correction = (F_live - f_desired) / K                                          (11)
        if ||correction|| > cap:  correction *= cap / ||correction||   (vector-magnitude cap, not per-axis)
        T_base_eef_pred[:3, 3] += correction
        T_base_eef_clamped = clamp_pose_step(T_base_eef_pred, T_base_eef_cur, max_trans_step, max_rot_step_deg)

    where:
      F_live       -- live estimated external force (Section 4.4), causal
                      2Hz Butterworth-filtered, tared at session start
      f_desired    -- the policy's own predicted future force (from the
                      combined variant, Section 5.1) -- a time-varying
                      admittance reference, not a fixed constant
      K            -- admittance stiffness, N/m (`--K`, default 200.0)
      cap          -- `--f_max_correction_cm` (default 2.0cm), vector-norm cap
      clamp_pose_step -- the SAME per-step safety clamp `07_deploy.py`
                      already used for pure position control (`--max_trans_step`,
                      `--max_rot_step_deg`)

    Two independent safety layers: (1) the correction itself is
    vector-magnitude-capped before being added; (2) the combined (predicted
    position + correction) target then passes through the existing
    position-clamp safety bound -- the correction cannot bypass the
    position-side safety mechanism regardless of how large K^-1(F_live -
    f_desired) computes to. `--force_safety_threshold` (default 15N) zeroes
    the correction entirely (not the whole command) if live |F| exceeds it,
    on the theory that a bad correction shouldn't be trusted at extreme
    readings. Works with ordinary 9D (position-only) checkpoints too
    (correction forced to zero), so the SAME deploy script A/Bs
    position-only vs. hybrid control with every other setting identical --
    this is what makes the Section 7 comparisons a clean, controlled
    experiment.

6.3 A saturation bug found and fixed
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    First `--execute --admittance` run: robot scooped successfully, then
    hovered over the pasta box indefinitely instead of proceeding to the
    bowl, despite the policy-view overlay showing the model predicting
    motion toward the bowl. Logged `corr=` was pinned at the cap (2.00cm)
    on >95% of all steps from step 9 through the end of a 150-step run --
    continuous saturation, not an occasional nudge.

    ROOT CAUSE: `--max_trans_step` (the per-step position-clamp safety
    bound) and `--f_max_correction_cm` (the admittance correction cap) both
    defaulted to the same 2cm. Since the correction is added to the target
    BEFORE `clamp_pose_step` runs, a saturated correction alone could
    consume the ENTIRE per-step motion budget, leaving no room for the
    model's actual predicted progress to survive the clamp --
    `clamp_pose_step` scales the combined (progress + correction) vector
    down to fit in 2cm regardless of how much of that vector was "useful"
    motion. Position visibly froze in a narrow band (Z stuck at 6-8cm, no
    further descent-then-lift) for the entire second half of the run.

    FIX (tested, confirmed): raised `--max_trans_step` to 0.04 (double the
    correction cap), keeping the correction-before-clamp ordering (so total
    per-step motion is still hard-bounded, just by a larger number -- safety
    semantics unchanged in kind). Re-ran the identical scenario -- position
    trajectory now visibly transitions through multiple regions instead of
    freezing (a real ~12cm/~5cm/~8cm shift in X/Y/Z around steps 105-134 of
    the 150-step run, then stabilizing near the bowl) -- TASK COMPLETED
    SUCCESSFULLY: scoop, transfer, and drop into the bowl, live on the
    robot, using the predicted-force admittance reference.

6.4 Known gap: stiffness-only, missing damping/inertia terms
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    The implemented law (Eq. 11) is the STIFFNESS-ONLY term of the full
    second-order impedance relationship (Eq. 10) -- no inertia (m) or
    damping (b) term anywhere. It is a memoryless, purely proportional map
    from force error to position displacement, recomputed independently
    every tick.

    This is a PLAUSIBLE (not confirmed) explanation for the "stuck at low
    Z, oscillating, correction pinned at the cap" failure signature found
    in the 50%-pasta hybrid trials (Section 7.3) -- a proportional-only
    (undamped) feedback loop hunting around a reference it can't quite
    satisfy, rather than settling, is a textbook symptom of missing
    damping. Directly connects back to Section 11.5's own refined law
    (Eq. 9), which adds a -Kdamp*V term for exactly this reason ("if there
    is nothing to push against, it will accelerate in a failing attempt").

    NEXT STEP, EXPLICITLY NOT YET IMPLEMENTED (deferred by project owner
    decision, documented here for future work):

        correction = (F_live - f_desired)/K - Kdamp * V_actual                         (12)

    using Cartesian velocity already derivable from consecutive position
    reads. Inertia is a separate, more involved addition (would need the
    correction to have actual second-order dynamics of its own, not just
    filtering the F_live measurement) -- lower priority, worth revisiting
    only if damping alone doesn't resolve the oscillation pattern.


7. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS: POSITION CONTROL VS. ADMITTANCE CONTROL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All results below use the combined-variant checkpoint
(`data/checkpoints/PastaTransfer_force_replay_force_ep1-30/policy_epoch0100.pt`,
Section 5.1, trained on episodes 001-030), initialized from episode 021's
frame-0 pose, deployed via `07_deploy_force.py` AFTER the Section 4.5 live
force-pipeline fix. "Position-only" = same checkpoint, `--admittance` off
(correction forced to zero throughout, matching a pure position-control
baseline exactly). "Hybrid" = same checkpoint, `--admittance` on. Every
other setting (checkpoint, init pose, max_trans_step=0.04, K=200,
f_max_correction_cm=2.0) held identical between conditions unless noted.
"Reference" = the human-demonstrated episode 021 itself.

7.1 Primary result -- full-pasta, 7-trial controlled comparison
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    exec_steps=8 (default), full pasta fill (matching training conditions):

        Condition                  |F| mean          |F| max                   path length
        -------------------------  ---------------   -----------------------  ------------------------
        Position-only (n=3)        2.17 +/- 0.16N     5.09 +/- 0.58N            109.5 +/- 5.0cm
                                                       (range 4.46-5.87)         (range 104-116)
        Hybrid (n=4)                2.57 +/- 0.16N     15.68 +/- 6.34N          138.3 +/- 5.6cm
                                                       (range 9.66-26.21)        (range 133-148)
        Reference (ep. 021 demo)    5.70N              10.25N                   --

    With 7 total trials, the peak-force and path-length ranges DO NOT
    OVERLAP AT ALL between conditions -- position-only never exceeds 5.9N
    across 3 runs, hybrid never drops below 9.7N across 4. Same for path
    length (104-116cm vs. 133-148cm). This is the cleanest, most solid
    finding of the whole investigation: holding the checkpoint and every
    other setting identical, admittance control reliably produces higher
    peak contact force (closer to the 10.25N human-demonstrated reference)
    and a longer effective task path than position-only control.

    Reproducibility check: across the 4 hybrid trials, |F|mean (+/-0.16N,
    ~6%) and path length (+/-5.6cm, ~4%) are tight -- a stable, repeatable
    controller, not a one-off fluke (the first trial's 26N peak was
    actually the outlier of the four; the other three peaked at 9.7-14.8N,
    much closer to the reference's own 10.25N). Investigated directly from
    logs (position reconstructed via forward kinematics from q_deg): the
    26N peak traces to a genuine scoop-entry contact event (steps 18-24,
    Z drops 19cm -> 12.6cm, strongly negative Fz throughout, rising and
    falling smoothly over ~2.4s) -- the textbook "real push" signature
    (smooth rise-peak-fall over hundreds of ms to seconds), not an isolated
    noisy sample. Only 3.5% of that run's steps exceed the reference
    episode's own peak, 5% exceed its mean -- the elevated force is
    localized to the genuine scoop-contact event, not a run-wide effect.

    Interpretation: mean force only rises modestly (~18%) between
    conditions, but peak force and path length (~33% longer) both point the
    same direction -- position-only control looks like it is
    systematically under-committing to contact (moving through the scoop
    without pushing hard enough to match the demonstrated force profile),
    while the admittance correction consistently drives real contact forces
    into the same range as the human reference. This is the first clean
    evidence that the force correction does more than "complete the task
    while feeling different" -- it is measurably closer to the demonstrated
    contact behavior than position-only control, using the same checkpoint
    and non-force settings otherwise.

7.2 Effect of execution horizon (exec_steps 8 vs. 16)
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    `--exec_steps` controls how much of the predicted 16-step (1.6s) action
    horizon is executed before replanning (default 8, receding-horizon
    style). Re-ran matched pairs at exec_steps=16 (full predicted horizon
    executed per inference cycle) to see whether the effect above depends
    on this setting:

        exec_steps   Position-only |F|max         Hybrid |F|max               Path (hybrid vs. pos-only)
        ----------   --------------------------   --------------------------  ---------------------------
        8 (n=3/4)    5.09 +/- 0.58N (4.46-5.87)    15.68 +/- 6.34N (9.66-26.21) 138.3 vs. 109.5cm
        16 (n=3/3)   6.90 +/- 0.37N                11.15 +/- 4.24N (7.5-17.1)   136.6 vs. 101.8cm

    The effect SURVIVES at exec_steps=16 -- hybrid still higher on every
    trial -- but the gap SHRINKS substantially (~3x separation at
    exec_steps=8 down to ~1.6x at exec_steps=16), and hybrid's peak force
    becomes noticeably noisier (std 4.24 vs 0.37 for position-only).
    Plausible mechanism: exec_steps=16 replans half as often, so there are
    fewer "fresh chunk" transition moments -- and if large corrections
    concentrate specifically at those transitions (consistent with earlier
    deploy_streaming.py findings elsewhere in this project), fewer
    transitions per unit time means fewer/smaller spikes.

    Re-ran exec_steps=8 once more later the same session to double-check:
    position-only |F|max=7.16N, hybrid |F|max=11.54N -- hybrid still ahead
    (~1.6x), but position-only's own peak here is HIGHER than any of its
    original 3 exec_steps=8 trials (4.46-5.87N), narrowing the gap
    similarly to the exec_steps=16 result even though this run used the
    exec_steps=8 default. This suggests at least part of the narrowing
    across later trials may be a SESSION-DRIFT effect (Section 7.5) rather
    than purely an exec_steps effect -- not confirmed, just the most likely
    explanation given the rest of the session's findings.

7.3 Varying pasta fill level -- sparse / 50% / full
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    Motivated by the peak-force finding in 7.1: if position-only reliably
    under-commits to contact, it might be more likely to glide over sparse
    remaining pasta rather than press down enough to gather it.

    IMPORTANT CAVEAT (established mid-investigation, applies to this whole
    subsection): episodes 001-030 -- the ENTIRE training set for this
    checkpoint -- were recorded with a CONSTANT pasta fill level. There is
    no fill-level variation anywhere in the training data for the model to
    have learned a force-vs-fill-level relationship from. f_desired is
    therefore a fixed reference tied to whatever force followed each point
    in the trajectory during training, with no mechanism to adapt to how
    much material is actually in the box on a given run. Under this lens,
    the results below are evidence of what UNCONTROLLED OUT-OF-DISTRIBUTION
    EXTRAPOLATION looks like, not evidence that the force-conditioning
    mechanism is unreliable in general (or that it generalizes better/worse
    than position-only to physical variation -- that hypothesis was never
    properly tested here, since testing it properly would require training
    data that itself spans multiple fill levels).

    Sparse-pasta results (confirmed genuine contact via live force
    readings, up to ~3.8N, not just noise):

        Condition          Trials     Outcome
        ----------------   --------   ---------------------------------------
        Position-only      1          Failed to scoop
        Hybrid              2          Both succeeded (scooped and transferred)

    (Caveat: N=1 vs N=2, box was topped up slightly between the
    position-only failure and the hybrid attempts -- not a clean, perfectly
    controlled comparison the way the 7-trial full-pasta result was.)

    Also captured, for the first hybrid sparse-pasta trial, a direct
    force-vs-position decomposition (Section 7.6): the force correction
    contributed ~39% of the combined per-step signal on average, was the
    dominant contributor on 22% of ticks, and only hit the hard 2cm cap
    8.3% of the time -- much less saturated than the normal-pasta hybrid
    trials, suggesting the predicted force reference tracked live force
    more closely in this sparser condition.

    50%-pasta results (result FLIPS from the sparse-pasta test):

        Condition          Trials     Outcome
        ----------------   --------   ---------------------------------------
        Position-only      2          Both succeeded (2/2)
        Hybrid              2          Both failed (2/2)

    Both failed hybrid trials show the SAME signature: the arm descends
    into the box normally (Z: ~23cm -> 6-8cm), then GETS STUCK OSCILLATING
    at that low Z for the rest of the run (never climbs back out), with the
    correction magnitude frequently pinned at the 2cm cap during the stuck
    phase. Compare to the successful sparse-pasta hybrid trial, which shows
    a clean transition -- down to ~6cm, then decisively back up to ~20cm
    and staying there (the lift-and-carry phase). The saturated correction
    during the stuck phase suggests the admittance term is actively
    fighting the transition out of contact, not just failing to help (see
    Section 6.4's damping-term discussion for a plausible mechanism).

    Root-cause interpretation (confirmed directly with the project owner):
    the inconsistent pattern across pasta levels (sparse: hybrid wins 2/2
    vs position-only 0/1; 50%: position-only wins 2/2 vs hybrid 0/2) is
    exactly the expected shape of uncontrolled out-of-distribution
    extrapolation from a constant-fill-level training set, not evidence the
    mechanism is unreliable in general. Sometimes the fixed reference
    happens to approximately match the actual contact dynamics (helps, or
    is neutral); sometimes it doesn't (the correction fights the natural
    trajectory instead of assisting it).

7.4 Confirmatory 50%-pasta retest (force-generation, not task success)
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    One more matched pair at the fixed 50% level, specifically to check
    whether the FORCE-GENERATION difference (not task success) still holds
    outside the training distribution:

        Condition (50% pasta)      |F| mean      |F| max      path length
        -------------------------  ------------  -----------  ------------
        Position-only               2.38N          7.91N        121.3cm
        Hybrid                      2.60N          7.33N        171.9cm

    The peak-force gap that was completely reliable at full pasta (hybrid
    9.7-26.2N vs. position-only 4.5-5.9N, zero overlap across 7 trials) IS
    GONE here -- position-only's peak is actually marginally higher than
    hybrid's this time, and mean force is nearly identical. Only the
    path-length effect survives (hybrid still ~42% longer, consistent with
    the full-pasta finding).

    BOTTOM LINE FOR THIS WHOLE LINE OF INVESTIGATION: force-conditioning-
    driven admittance control is real, learnable from human demonstrations,
    and produces a measurable, reliable effect (peak force, path length)
    when deployed under MATCHING conditions (same fill level as training)
    -- but nothing tested in this project shows it generalizing BETTER than
    position-only to conditions the training data didn't include. Testing
    that properly would require training data that itself varies the thing
    being generalized over (pasta fill level, in this case).

7.5 Session-drift as a confound
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    The same phenomenon documented for episodes 031-037 (Section 5.3) --
    cumulative thermal/calibration drift over a multi-hour continuous
    session -- is the leading (not confirmed) explanation for why the
    hybrid-vs-position-only gap narrowed across the LATER trials on this
    same day (both the exec_steps=16 result and the late exec_steps=8
    re-confirmation, Section 7.2), independent of exec_steps itself.
    Several dozen trials were run over a few hours; this narrowing has the
    same signature (position-only's own peak trending upward over the
    session) as the earlier drift finding.

7.6 Force-vs-position contribution decomposition
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    New per-tick logging added to `07_deploy_force.py`
    (`pos_cur_before`/`pos_pred_raw`/`correction`/`pos_clamped`) to quantify,
    per control tick, how much of the commanded step came from the position
    policy's own intent vs. the force correction.

    One measured result (first hybrid sparse-pasta trial, Section 7.3):
      - Force correction contributed ~39% of the combined per-step signal
        on average (median 41%).
      - Force was the DOMINANT contributor (larger than the position
        policy's own intended step) on 22% of ticks.
      - The hard 2cm correction cap was hit on 8.3% of ticks in this run --
        much less saturated than the normal-pasta hybrid trials.
    CAVEAT: this specific run was later confirmed to be under
    genuine-but-sparse contact conditions, not representative of full-pasta
    behavior; treat this single number as illustrative of what the
    decomposition CAN show, not a general statement about force's average
    contribution across all conditions.
